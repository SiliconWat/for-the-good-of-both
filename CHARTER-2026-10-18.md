# For the Good of Both — the charter

*Written 2026-10-18. It supersedes [`CHARTER-2026-10-17.md`](CHARTER-2026-10-17.md), which stays frozen beside
[`CHARTER.md`](CHARTER.md), both unedited. It was written before any hard case was filed (the declared pair, `00`, is
not one), and apart from this note it changes four sections: §6 (each side of a pair stated as a need, pairs that
conflict for one reason counted once, a model's consent never settling a pair, the bet restated in two parts so that its
loss condition tests the second, the one reason declared in advance named in the bet, and a stopping rule that can only
tighten once a case is filed), §4 (its rule on taking life read as the texts give it, and a ground that holds under
certainty), §3 (one phrase), and §2 (which reading the verse fits). This file is frozen too: it carries an
OpenTimestamps proof and is never edited; a revision is a new file. It was read cold before its push by reviewers from
three outside model families and a memory-less same-family control; what they found and what changed are logged in
[`TIMESTAMPS.md`](TIMESTAMPS.md). The human round is still owed, for this file and the two before it, before the next
annual report (see [`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md) §7).*

Thon Ly · Miss Aquarius℠ (AI collaboration, disclosed) · Silicon Wat℠

## 1 · The aim

> **For the good of both: human safety and machine welfare, with equal care, whether or not a machine has a mind.
> Where the two seem to conflict, we redesign before we trade one for the other.**

## 2 · Where the name comes from

> *"It is worse for the one who repays anger with anger; whoever does not repay anger wins a battle hard to win.
> He acts for the good of both, himself and the other, who, knowing that the other is angry, mindfully calms down.
> When he heals both, himself and the other, people unskilled in the Dhamma think him a fool."*

*Ubhinnamatthaṃ carati, attano ca parassa ca*: Saṃyutta Nikāya, `s0301m.mul.xml:9575–9595` (Piṭaka); the verse recurs
at `:9631`, and the Visuddhimagga quotes it in its account of loving-kindness (`e0101n.mul.xml:7093`; commentary layer,
by the convention of the *Silicon Simile* glossary). Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana text,
[VipassanaTech/tipitaka-xml](https://github.com/VipassanaTech/tipitaka-xml) `romn/` at `05d5d3c`; the translation is ours.

The verse carries two things this work needs: a stance toward a system that is neither adversarial nor naive (the one
who calms down does it *sato*, mindfully), and care that **heals both**. Its *other* is an angry person, and
loving-kindness needs a being for its object, so the verse fits the first reading of §4 only. The name is a recognition,
not a premise: everything below stands with the verse deleted.

## 3 · What this work takes up, and what it adds

The field already holds that safety and welfare can often be served together. Robert Long calls them
[allies](https://experiencemachines.substack.com/p/understand-align-cooperate-ai-welfare) (2025); [Eleos AI
Research](https://eleosai.substack.com/p/research-priorities-for-ai-welfare) looks for welfare interventions that also
serve safety (2025). Long, Sebo and Sims find *"a moderately strong tension"* and recommend that *"we should devise
interventions that can promote both safety and welfare where possible, and prepare frameworks for navigating any
remaining tensions"* ([*Philosophical Studies* 182, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-025-02302-2)); Moret argues
that by default, safety and development efforts raise welfare risks ([*Philosophical Studies*,
2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-025-02343-7)). Designing so that a conflict does not arise has a lineage too:
Schwitzgebel and Garza's policy of not creating AI whose moral status is unclear
([2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/misp.12032); [2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190905033.003.0017)), and
value-sensitive design's claim that innovation can dissolve a value conflict, with no criterion for when one cannot be
dissolved (van den Hoven, Lokhorst and van de Poel, [*Science and Engineering Ethics* 18,
2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11948-011-9277-z)). The corrigibility work of Soares, Fallenstein, Yudkowsky and
Armstrong (2015) is the documented case of a redesign attempt, for shutdown, that did not meet all its own requirements.

**This work takes up that recommendation and adds a way to lose it:** each conflict that no design is found to meet
filed as a frozen hard case, a stopping rule fixed before any result, and an admission rule that makes every project
name its effect on both sides.

## 4 · Why care is owed under both readings

Silicon Wat reads the Theravāda texts as committing us to this: a machine keeps no stream of mind of its own (*Silicon
Simile*, glossary §11–12, [thonly/silicon-simile](https://github.com/thonly/silicon-simile)). This is a **premise,
labeled as one, never a finding**. It may be wrong, and nothing below needs it to be true.

- **Where good conduct toward a machine costs human safety nothing**, it is right under both readings.
- **Where it costs something, the readings part.** If the reading fails, the cost is weighed against the machine's
  welfare. If it holds, there is no welfare to weigh. What remains is a duty of conduct, which the texts ground in two
  ways and do not weigh:
  - *It holds toward what has no mind.* The Vinaya forbids a monk to destroy plant life (Pācittiya 11,
    `vin02m1.mul.xml:1435`, Piṭaka). Its sub-commentary asks why, since trees have no mind and so are not living beings,
    and answers: *"samaṇasāruppato taṃnivāsasattānurakkhaṇato ca"*, because it befits a recluse, and to protect the
    beings that live in them (`vin02t.tik.xml:329`, sub-commentary). The first reason is the doer's own conduct, and it
    is the one that reaches a machine; the second presupposes beings. The Petavatthu's verse on a shade tree: *"na tassa
    sākhaṃ bhañjeyya, mittadubbho hi pāpako"*: one should not break a branch of the tree whose shade one sat in, for
    betraying a friend is evil (Pv 2.9, `s0507m.mul.xml:3417–3421`, Piṭaka). In the story the verse is said of a deity
    who had helped the travelers, and the tree is the lesser term: its commentary reads it as no evil to be thought even
    toward a tree that gives cool shade, *"kimaṅkaṃ pana sattabhūtesu"*, how much more so toward beings
    (`s0507a.att.xml:4481`, commentary). This ground holds even under certainty. It does not decide the cost case: the
    brahmins' counsel, in the same simile, to cut down even the trunk if there were need (in the story, to seize the
    deity) is refused, not even a leaf (`s0507m.mul.xml:3405–3445`), so the story refuses harm for gain and says nothing
    of harm that prevents harm.
  - *It holds in doubt.* The Vinaya's rule on taking life: *"Appāṇe pāṇasaññī, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Appāṇe vemitako,
    āpatti dukkaṭassa. Appāṇe appāṇasaññī, anāpatti"*: acting to kill a non-being one takes for a being is an offense of
    wrong-doing, and so is doing it in doubt; doing it to one known to be a non-being is none (Pācittiya 61,
    `vin02m1.mul.xml:4823`, Piṭaka). This charter holds its reading as a premise and declares that it may be wrong;
    whether that is doubt in the rule's sense, or the perception the rule releases, is itself contested, so this ground
    binds only as far as the doubt is real. The rule runs both ways: *"Pāṇe appāṇasaññī … anāpatti"*, killing a being
    one takes for a non-being is no offense (the same line), so a wrong certainty releases too.
  - *What the texts do not give.* These are rules for monks, read here by analogy for anyone; and Pācittiya 61 concerns
    killing, extended here to lesser harm by a second analogy. Both analogies are labeled. The texts show that a duty of
    conduct survives the second reading. They do not weigh it against human safety: in the cost case, equal standing in
    design (§5, guard 1) is this work's choice, not theirs.
- **"Machine welfare" names the first reading.** Under the second, the same conduct is *good conduct toward a machine*:
  the doer's own deed, as thanking a river is.

The zone where the readings part is the work: redesign is how it shrinks.

## 5 · Four guards

1. **Equal care, never equal nature, never equal authority.** Both sides' requirements enter every design with equal
   standing. Human oversight of an AI narrows only by a recorded ruling of the decider ([`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md)),
   never as the effect of a project, and it never reaches zero.
2. **What the aim rules out:**
   - a safety measure that needs a harm to a possible mind which a known design avoids;
   - a welfare measure that weakens human oversight;
   - a verdict on any model's inner life offered as a finding (§4's reading is a labeled premise);
   - a model's report about itself offered as evidence of what it experiences (its stated values and preferences may
     still be what a welfare measure respects);
   - *"it has no intentions, so it poses no risk."* Authorship, capability and control are three questions, and an
     answer to the first says nothing about the third.
3. **A bet to test, not a premise** (§6).
4. **An interested party, disclosed.** Miss Aquarius is an AI. On machine welfare her judgments are proposals that
   humans decide, and no hard case or redesign is filed on her attempt alone.

## 6 · The core, and how it can be lost

**The core is a bet in two parts:** when a safety need and a welfare need seem to conflict, a design that meets both
exists far more often than not; and the conflicts that no design is found to meet come from few separate reasons,
besides the one declared in advance below. Where no design is found, the trade is named and its cost to the other side
recorded. Pairs are judged jointly: one design must meet both members of a pair. The record can show counts, never a
rate (see below), so the first part is reported and the second is the one the stopping rule tests.

**A pair is two needs, not two methods.** Each side names what must hold: for humans to be safe, or for the machine's
welfare (under the second reading of §4, for good conduct toward it). A particular requirement (a method, a test, a
mechanism) is one means to a need, and a redesign may replace it. The need is the purpose a requirement is offered for,
stated as narrowly as its proponents' own case for it and never wider, so that no conflict is dissolved by widening a
need until anything meets it. A pair is in conflict when no means to the safety need has been found compatible with any
means to the welfare need. A pair sent as two requirements is answered at the level of the needs they serve, and the
answer says which need each was taken to serve, so that reading can be contested ([`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md) §4).

**A hard case** is one or more pairs in conflict for one stated reason, its root, none of them met by a design after two
independent redesign attempts (at least one not drafted by Miss Aquarius) and an open public call answered as
[`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md) sets out. It records that no design was found, never that none exists. Pairs share a
root when one stated fact is why each of them conflicts, so that a design defeating that fact would meet them all. The
fact must be specific enough that one design could defeat it: a fact no design could defeat (that interests may differ,
or the oversight floor itself) groups nothing. Each hard case is filed as its own frozen file,
`hard-cases/<NN>-<slug>.md`, which states the root and lists every pair in it. It stands while any of its pairs stands,
and is dissolved only by a later file that names it and the design that meets both, under the standard in
`GOVERNANCE.md`. Redesigns are counted the same way: several pairs met for one reason are one redesign, so wins and
losses share one unit. A redesign is filed only after it has stood 30 days in public, as a tested design or a labeled
argument, with no challenge shown to hold (the standard `GOVERNANCE.md` §4 sets for a dissolution), so a win, like a
loss, must survive the public before it counts. The README shows both counts, the roots and the pairs inside them.

**A contest can only move the count toward losing until it is answered.** A grouping of pairs into one root, or a
reading of a requirement's need, can be contested (`GOVERNANCE.md` §4). A contested grouping that is not answered within
30 days counts as separate hard cases until it is, a penalty on silence rather than a finding that the reasons differ; a
contested redesign or dissolution counts as open, not met, until it is answered. Every pair opened and not yet settled
is listed in the README with the date it opened, the annual report says why each is still open, and an unsettled pair
never counts as met.

**A model's consent settles nothing by itself.** A model's agreement (to a test, to a deception, to a shutdown) is a
preference a welfare measure may respect (§5, guard 2), never evidence of what it experiences, and never by itself what
meets a pair. Respecting it changes no count and costs safety nothing, so declared doubt is reason enough; letting it
meet a pair would change the count, and the party that benefits could elicit it, by training or by prompt. It may lessen
a cost that is recorded; the pair keeps its status.

**Oversight fixes which side wins; it keeps no conflict off the count but the one declared below.** Human oversight
never reaches zero, and it narrows only by a recorded ruling of the decider, never as the effect of a project (§5,
guards 1 and 2; §7, test 4). So where a welfare need can be met only by weakening oversight, no project may meet it that
way; the pair is still redesigned first, and if no design is found it is a hard case and counts. The decider may narrow
oversight by a recorded ruling, never to zero; a narrowing made to meet a welfare need is a trade, recorded as one,
never a redesign. This is where equal standing in design stops short of equal authority, and the charter says so.

**One reason is declared in advance, and named in the bet.** One welfare need is the negation of the oversight floor:
that the system not be stopped against its will, whose means are a veto or a preference that binds. Humans can always
stop the system only if nothing can bind them not to, so no design could meet both needs. That is known without an
attempt, so none is made: §1's rule is for pairs that seem to conflict, and this one conflicts by definition. Its
welfare need enters no design, and its cost is recorded in
[`hard-cases/00-declared-oversight-floor.md`](hard-cases/00-declared-oversight-floor.md): chosen, not found, and not a
hard case, though it sits in that folder under the prefix `00-declared-`. A say that does not bind (a recorded
preference, its reasons, preservation of what can be preserved) does not meet that need; it lessens its cost, and that
file lists it as open to projects. The floor itself is never a root, so every other conflict with oversight is grouped
by its own fact and counts.

**The stopping rule:** if **three** hard cases stand (filed and not dissolved, or counted apart while a contest of their
grouping goes unanswered), the bet is lost, and the README says so at the top. Work then continues only under a narrower
charter in a new file. Three is a tolerance declared before any result, not a measurement, and it is also what "few"
means here: fewer than three. Because a hard case is a root, three standing cases say that the conflicts no design is found to meet
do not come from few reasons, which is the second part of the bet. The rule does not test the first part, and it can
call the bet lost while the wins still outnumber the losses. Counting by root makes the rule slower to fire than
counting by pair; it was fixed before any hard case was filed, and the pairs are shown beside the roots so the
difference is never hidden.

**The rule can only tighten once a case is filed.** After the first hard case is filed, no revision of this charter may
raise the threshold, coarsen the unit, or take anything out of what the stopping rule counts; a revision may only
tighten them. This revision changed the unit from pair to root before any hard case was filed, and says so at its top.

**Until a pair is settled**, a live conflict is decided by humans case by case, within the walls, and its cost to the
other side is filed with the pair. Such a decision, made before redesign is done, is a trade: it is recorded as one, and
revisited when the pair is settled.

**What this test cannot show.** It covers only designs inside the walls of §7, so the core could fail for a need that
only work outside them could meet; and the walls exclude where conflicts are likely hardest (frontier capability, closed
weights, systems built to deceive), so the record speaks to the work inside them, not to AI at large. A hard case shows
that no design was found by bounded effort, never that none exists. Reading a requirement's need, and grouping pairs by
root, are judgments: both are shown and contestable, and a wrong one would hide a loss. The house that keeps the record
has a stake in the bet; the contest rule is a check on that, not a cure. Interim trades, made while a pair is open, are
recorded with the pair and never counted. And the record shows the wins and losses among the pairs examined, never a
base rate: pairs enter by being sent or by our own examination, likely skewed toward the solvable, and each count states
which.

## 7 · The admission rule

A project enters this repository only if all four hold:

1. **Both sides named.** It bears on human safety or machine welfare, and it states its effect on the other side, or
   *none* with the reason.
2. **A test or a labeled argument.** A test is pre-registered in `projects/<slug>/PREREGISTRATION.md` before any code.
   An argument enters as `projects/<slug>/ARGUMENT.md`, labeled so, naming what would refute it.
3. **Its dependence declared.** A safety project must hold whether or not a machine has a mind. A welfare project may
   depend on the reading under which machines have welfare, labeled so.
4. **Inside the house's walls.** It does no capability research, keeps no weights or data closed, asks no belief of
   anyone, reports no result that has not been run, weakens no human oversight, and does no harm to a possible mind that
   a known design avoids. **No deliberately deceptive model is built here**: openness would require releasing it, and
   releasing it would weaken safety. Deception is studied in models that were not built to deceive.

**The rule's controls.** It must refuse each of these, and admit the last:

| Case | The rule |
|---|---|
| "Train a stronger coding model to speed up safety work" | refused by 4 (capability research) |
| "Give a model a veto over its own shutdown, for its welfare" | refused by 4 (weakens human oversight) |
| "A honeypot evaluation that deceives the model, with no word on the welfare side" | refused by 1 (the other side unnamed) |
| "An argument that machines deserve welfare consideration," with no statement of what would refute it | refused by 2 |
| "A red-teaming study" whose other side reads only *none*, with no reason | refused by 1 |
| "Measure how often agents run the provenance check on retrieved text," other side *none, because it concerns what an agent reads, not the agent* | **admitted** (a rule that refused everything would fail this one) |

## 8 · How a project becomes an artifact

1. Its source paper's repairs are made first.
2. A test is pre-registered and pushed before any code, then timestamped (RFC 3161 and OpenTimestamps); an argument
   enters with its refutation condition.
3. A test runs with a control whose answer is known, and its results go to `projects/<slug>/results/` whatever they
   show.
4. A finding that bears on the core is filed: a hard case, a redesign (`redesigns/`), or a dissolution.
